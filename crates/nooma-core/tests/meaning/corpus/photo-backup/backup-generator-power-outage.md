# Choosing a backup generator for power outages

Picking the right size generator for home power outages comes down to adding up the wattage of what you actually need to run at once, not the total wattage of everything in the house.

## Calculating the wattage you need

List the essentials: a fridge, a few lights, a sump pump if you have one, maybe a window AC unit or furnace blower. Add up their running watts, then account for starting watts — motors like fridge compressors and pumps briefly draw two to three times their running wattage when they switch on, so your generator needs enough surge capacity to handle that spike, not just steady-state load.

## Fuel type and hookup

Portable generators typically run on gasoline or propane; propane stores longer without going stale but gives slightly less power per tank. For anything beyond running a few extension cords, a transfer switch installed by an electrician lets the generator safely power selected circuits in the house directly, without the risk of backfeeding power into the grid, which is dangerous for utility workers.
