# Keeping a sourdough starter alive

A sourdough starter is a living culture of wild yeast and bacteria, and it stays healthy as long as it's fed regularly with fresh flour and water. Left too long without feeding, it runs out of food and turns sluggish, but a simple routine keeps it active for years.

## Feeding routine

If you bake often, feed the starter once a day: discard all but a couple of tablespoons, then mix in equal parts (by weight) flour and water. Room temperature works best for daily feeding — the starter should roughly double in size within four to eight hours and be full of bubbles. If you bake only occasionally, keep the starter in the fridge and feed it once a week instead; it will be sluggish there, which is normal.

## Signs it's healthy

A healthy starter smells pleasantly sour, sometimes like yogurt or ripe fruit, and rises predictably after feeding. A thin layer of grey liquid ("hooch") on top is harmless — just stir it back in or pour it off before feeding. Before baking, feed it a few hours ahead so it's active and bubbly, and do the float test: drop a small spoonful in water; if it floats, it's ready to use.
