# Preparing for a first half-marathon

Going from casual running to covering 21 kilometres comes down to building mileage gradually over several months rather than any single hard workout — most training plans for a first half-marathon run 10 to 12 weeks and assume you can already comfortably run 5 kilometres.

## Structuring the weeks

A typical week has three to four runs: one long run that grows by roughly a kilometre or two each week, one or two easy shorter runs, and optionally a slightly faster tempo run. Every third or fourth week, cut the long run distance back to let your body absorb the training rather than just piling on fatigue — skipping these recovery weeks is a common reason people get injured before race day.

## Tapering before race day

In the final two weeks, reduce total mileage significantly, especially the long run, while keeping some shorter, easy runs to stay loose. This taper feels counterintuitive since you'll have energy to spare, but it lets your legs recover fully so you arrive at the start line fresh rather than carrying accumulated fatigue from months of training.
