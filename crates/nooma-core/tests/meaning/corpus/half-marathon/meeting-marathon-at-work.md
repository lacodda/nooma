# Surviving a marathon of back-to-back meetings

A day packed with six or seven meetings in a row, with no gaps between them, leaves most people mentally drained by mid-afternoon and unable to remember what was actually decided in the earlier ones.

## Protecting small gaps

Where you can, push back on scheduling meetings truly back-to-back — even five minutes between calls is enough to stand up, get water, and mentally close out the previous conversation before opening the next one. Many calendar tools now let you automatically shorten default meeting lengths to 25 or 50 minutes instead of 30 or 60, which builds in a buffer without anyone having to ask for it.

## Keeping track of what actually happened

Take brief notes during each meeting, even just three or four bullet points on decisions and owners, since memory blurs fast once the next meeting starts. At the end of a heavy day, skim back through the notes once to catch anything that needs a message sent before you forget who agreed to what. Declining or delegating meetings where your presence isn't essential is often more effective than trying to survive every single one.
