# Watering succulents in winter

Most succulents go semi-dormant in winter as daylight hours shrink and indoor temperatures drop, even next to a sunny window. Their growth slows dramatically, which means they need far less water than in spring and summer — overwatering during dormancy is the single most common way people kill them.

## How often to water

As a rough guide, water only when the soil is completely dry at least an inch or two down, and even then, cut the frequency to once every three to four weeks for most species, compared to weekly in summer. Use the soak-and-dry method: water thoroughly until it drains from the bottom, then let the soil dry out fully before watering again.

## Watching for trouble

In winter, mushy or translucent lower leaves that fall off easily are a sign of overwatering rather than the plant simply going dormant. Wrinkled, slightly deflated leaves, on the other hand, usually mean it's genuinely time for a drink. If your succulents sit somewhere cold and dim rather than a bright windowsill, lean toward watering even less — a dormant plant in low light barely uses any water at all.
