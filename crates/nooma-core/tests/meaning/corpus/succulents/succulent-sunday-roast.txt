# A succulent Sunday roast

Getting a roast that's genuinely succulent — juicy all the way through rather than dry at the edges — comes down to a few habits more than any single trick: starting with a good cut, not overcooking it, and letting it rest properly before carving.

## Cooking it right

Bring the meat to room temperature before it goes in the oven, since a cold centre cooks unevenly. Sear it first at high heat to build flavour on the outside, then finish at a lower temperature so the inside cooks gently and evenly. A meat thermometer takes the guesswork out entirely — pull it from the oven a few degrees before your target temperature, since it keeps cooking as it rests.

## Why resting matters

Cutting into meat straight out of the oven lets most of the juices run straight onto the board instead of staying in the meat. Tent it loosely with foil and let it rest for at least fifteen to twenty minutes for a larger joint — the muscle fibres relax and reabsorb the juices, which is what actually makes each slice taste succulent rather than just well-cooked.
